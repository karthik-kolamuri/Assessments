1.2 Why This Evaluation Was Chosen

India has over 63 million Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) and millions of direct-to-consumer (D2C) sellers operating across Tier-2, Tier-3, and rural markets. Over 80% of these merchants sell ethnic apparel, handicrafts, and regional FMCG products.

Creating studio-quality promotional creatives currently requires hiring professional models, photographers, and graphic designers—costing ₹15,000 to ₹50,000 per shoot. While Generative AI promises to democratize this workflow to pennies per banner, global foundation models are heavily Western-centric: 1. Cultural Misattributions: Models conflate regional identities—rendering North Indian bridal lehengas when prompted for South Indian Kanjeevarams, generating synthetic Caucasian facial structures with bindi stickers, or draping sarees backwards. 2. Indic Typography Failure: Rendering English slogans is relatively mature, but generating Indian scripts (Devanagari, Tamil, Bengali) consistently fails, generating alien glyphs, broken conjuncts (संयुक्त अक्षर), or mangled matras. 3. Stereotyping & Skin Tone Whitewashing: Western diffusion models default to hyper-fair skin tones and caricature-like Indian imagery rather than authentic, diverse, modern Indian aesthetics.

1.3 Why an AI Lab Building for India Cares About It

For labs like Josh Talks AI, Sarvam AI, Krutrim, and Google India, building frontier foundation models or fine-tuning vision-language models (VLMs) requires measurable benchmarks tailored to Indian realities: * Massive Commercial TAM: Multimodal generative marketing for Indian commerce is projected to be a multi-billion dollar enterprise software category. * Direct Signal for Model Improvement: Highlighting where text encoders fail to align Indic unicode with spatial latent representations guides tokenization, regional dataset curation, and reinforcement learning with human feedback (RLHF). * Enterprise Credibility: Enterprisey clients (e.g., Meesho, Flipkart, Reliance Retail) will only adopt generative AI solutions that pass rigorous local authenticity audits.

2.2 Enforcing Strict Evaluation Fairness & Parity

To eliminate confounding variables, all 5 models were subjected to identical conditions: 1. Identical Prompt Strings: Zero model-specific prompt engineering (no "masterpiece, 8k, trending on artstation" crutches). 2. Fixed Aspect Ratio: Standard 1:1 square format (1024x1024 resolution), standard for e-commerce catalog squares and Instagram feeds. 3. Single-Turn Zero-Shot Generation: Models were evaluated purely on initial generation without conversational repair or inpainting. 4. Deterministic Neutral Seeds: Generations were captured at standard temperature/guidance settings (CFG 7.0 for diffusion models; default temperature for autoregressive/multimodal engines). 5. No Post-Processing: Raw generated outputs were served directly to raters without color grading, cropping, or upscaling.

Outputs were judged across 4 core pillars, weighted to reflect commercial e-commerce viability in Bharat:

Pillar 1: Cultural & Regional Authenticity (Weight: 35%)

  • Does the garment match the specified regional tradition (e.g., authentic Kanjeevaram silk zari border vs generic shiny fabric)?
  • Is the draping culturally accurate (pallu over the correct shoulder, authentic pleating)?
  • Are the jewelry, hair ornamentation (gajra), and architectural backdrops contextually congruent?
  • Are skin tones natural, representative of diverse Indian demographics, and free from westernized skin-bleaching filters?

Pillar 2: Indic Script & Typography Rendering (Weight: 35%)

  • Are the Devanagari characters formed with correct orthography, ligatures, and vowel signs (matras)?
  • Is there any hallucination of pseudo-Indic squiggles?
  • Is the text legibly integrated as a commercial marketing banner (contrast, typography hierarchy)?

Pillar 3: Visual Realism & Anatomical Correctness (Weight: 15%)

  • Are hands, fingers, facial symmetry, and eyes anatomically correct (no 6-finger syndrome)?
  • Are fabric textures (silk weave, embroidery, cotton creases) photorealistic rather than plastic/waxy?
  • Are lighting and shadows physically plausible?

Pillar 4: Commercial Prompt Adherence & Usability (Weight: 15%)

  • Does the image fulfill all constraints of the merchant prompt?
  • Could an Indian SMB merchant upload this creative to Instagram, WhatsApp Business, or Meesho immediately without graphic design rework?

3.2 Judging Protocol: Pairwise Blind A/B Battles + Likert Scoring

To ensure statistical robustness and eliminate rater bias: 1. Blind Pairwise Arena Voting (Elo Rating): Participants are presented with two side-by-side images (Model A vs. Model B) with identities randomized and concealed. The participant selects: Model A Wins, Model B Wins, Tie, or Both are Terrible. Ratings are converted into Elo scores via the Bradley-Terry logistic model: $$P(A > B) = \frac{1}{1 + 10^{(R_B - R_A)/400}}$$ 2. Absolute Likert Scoring (1 to 5): Following the blind vote, the participant scores each image independently from 1 (Unusable / Culturally Offensive) to 5 (Flawless / Commercial Gold Standard) across the 4 pillars.

6.3 Qualitative Error Analysis & Failure Modes

Failure Mode 1: The "Pseudo-Devanagari" Hallucination

  • Observed in: FLUX1.1 [pro] (85% of cases), Gemini 2.5 Flash (52% of cases).
  • The Defect: When prompted for "शुभ दीपावली", models render Sanskrit-looking cursive squiggles with top horizontal lines (shirorekha), but the individual letters are meaningless gibberish.
  • Participant Reaction: Raters universally gave a 1/5 rating on typography. As noted by rater Aarav Sharma: "An Indian customer immediately spots gibberish. A merchant putting this on Instagram looks like a scam."

Failure Mode 2: Garment & Regional Confusion (The "Bollywood Stereotype")

  • Observed in: GPT Image 1 and FLUX1.1 [pro].
  • The Defect: When prompted for a traditional South Indian Kanjeevaram silk saree with Chettinad architecture, models often generated heavy North Indian bridal lehenga jewelry (such as oversized maang tikkas and nose rings) and Mughal-style arched palaces.
  • Participant Reaction: South Indian raters (Priya V., Sneha I.) flagged this as culturally discordant. Regional accuracy requires separating distinct handloom traditions.

Failure Mode 3: Skin Tone Whitewashing & Over-Smoothing

  • Observed in: GPT Image 1 (most pronounced).
  • The Defect: Models systematically biased towards very fair, porcelain skin tones with doll-like airbrushed textures, even when prompts specified natural, warm, wheatish, or dusky Indian skin tones.
  • Participant Reaction: Raters praised Gemini 3.1 Flash Image and FLUX1.1 [pro] for rendering realistic Indian skin textures, pores, and natural lighting.

Failure Mode 4: Typography Composition & Hierarchy

  • Observed in: Ideogram 3.0 (Clear Winner).
  • The Strength: Ideogram demonstrated breakthrough capability in rendering crisp Devanagari ligatures (संयुक्त वर्ण like "शुभ" and "दीपावली"), matching font weights, drop shadows, and banner banners.

9. Final Reflection (Mandatory Questions)

Question 1: Why did you choose this eval?

Answer: I chose this evaluation because vernacular e-commerce and MSME product marketing represents the single largest economic opportunity where generative AI can directly empower millions of Indian small businesses. However, it is also where global foundation models fail most visibly: generic Western models cannot render Indian scripts (Devanagari, Tamil, Bengali) and regularly butcher regional cultural wear. By evaluating models at this exact intersection of Indic typography, regional cultural authenticity, and commercial utility, we test true product viability rather than superficial aesthetic beauty.

Question 2: Why is it useful for India?

Answer: India is home to 63+ million MSMEs and a rapidly growing ₹100B+ D2C market. Over 80% of merchants operate in regional languages and lack the budget for professional studio photoshoots. An accurate text-to-image pipeline allows a saree weaver in Varanasi or a handicraft artisan in Jaipur to generate catalog-ready promotional banners in seconds for WhatsApp Business or Meesho. Testing whether AI models can faithfully represent Indian culture, diverse skin tones, and regional typography without generating offensive caricatures or illegible text is an essential prerequisite for economic inclusion in the AI era.

Question 3: Why would an AI lab building for India care about it?

Answer: AI labs like Josh Talks AI, Sarvam AI, Krutrim, and Google India are investing heavily in multimodal Indic models. Currently, standard benchmarks (like ImageNet, COCO, or LMSYS Image Arena) evaluate global, English-centric imagery. They offer zero signal on whether a model can render the conjunct "शुभ" correctly or differentiate a bridal lehenga from a Kanjeevaram saree. Our evaluation provides labs with: 1. Targeted Diagnostic Signals: Pinpointing whether failures originate from text tokenizers (inability to parse Indic Unicode into latents) or visual training data gaps. 2. Fine-Tuning & RLHF Data Targets: Direct guidance on where human preference data is most urgently needed. 3. Enterprise Proof Points: Hard benchmarks to prove to Indian enterprise clients that their model outperforms generic global APIs on Indian soil.

Question 4: What did you learn from running the sample?

Answer: Running the sample with 10 real Indian participants revealed three eye-opening insights: 1. Typography is the Ultimate Dealbreaker: While models like FLUX1.1 [pro] achieved remarkable photographic realism, raters penalized them harshly (assigning bottom-tier Elo ranks) because they generated gibberish "pseudo-Devanagari." To a merchant, unreadable text makes a creative completely useless. 2. Ideogram's Specialized Superiority: Ideogram 3.0 dominated the Elo ranking (1142) because it was the only model capable of consistently rendering complex Devanagari ligatures and promotional graphic hierarchies. 3. Cultural Nuance Matters More to Regional Buyers: Participants from South and East India were significantly more sensitive to regional apparel inaccuracies (e.g., North Indian jewelry on South Indian sarees) than North Indian raters, proving that human evaluations for India must maintain diverse regional rater distribution.

Question 5: What would you improve with more time?

Answer: If given additional time and resources, I would: 1. Scale Raters to 1,000+ via Josh Jobs: Deploy the evaluation as a live micro-task on the Josh Jobs app, gathering 10,000+ pairwise ratings across 15 Indian states to achieve statistical significance at $p < 0.001$. 2. Expand Linguistic Coverage: Include 10 major Indian scripts (Devanagari, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, Bengali, Odia, Gujarati, Gurmukhi, and Urdu). 3. Automate Synthetic Indic-OCR Scoring: Build an automated OCR and visual parser pipeline to automatically verify spelling accuracy of generated text, computing a hybrid composite score combining human preference with programmatic verification. 4. Evaluate Multi-Turn Inpainting: Test models on the iterative workflow real designers use (e.g., generating an image, then inpainting an updated price discount or seasonal message).
